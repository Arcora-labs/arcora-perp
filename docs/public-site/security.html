Security & Trust

This page states exactly what the current alpha guarantees, what it does not, and what secures your funds in between. We would rather you trust Arcora Perp for the right reasons than for vague ones.

What the zk proof guarantees

Every settled state root must satisfy the SP1 zkVM guest: the proof re-executes the committed window and the on-chain verifier rejects a transition that does not satisfy that program. All six commitment roots are derived inside the guest rather than accepted as arbitrary host-provided roots.

That is a transition-validity guarantee, not a complete operator-independence guarantee. The current alpha gateway holds server-custody spend keys and the oracle publisher authority. The proof does not independently establish that an operation reflects a user's intent when the gateway already possesses that user's custody key, and it does not establish external price truth when the accepted oracle transcript is signed by a gateway-controlled publisher key. Permissionless Merkle claims enforce the destination and amount committed by a settled withdrawals root; they do not erase those upstream trust assumptions.

What the TEE guarantees

Matching and ordering run inside an Intel TDX confidential VM. The TEE provides matching/ordering privacy and integrity attested to Intel TDX hardware, with the enclave identity (its signing key) sealed to the attested measurement. Signed receipts, the sealed order ingress, and shielded account state all hang off this identity.

Current gaps (testnet alpha)

Read this before depositing anything you care about

  1. The gateway is a trusted/custodial component in this alpha. It holds server-custody account spend keys in process memory and controls the oracle publisher key. A gateway compromise can therefore affect fund authorization and market inputs even though invalid guest transitions remain unprovable. This is the principal A10 trust boundary.
  2. The zk prover currently runs on a development workstation and is NOT yet TEE-attested. The witness leaves the sequencer enclave sealed, but the prover machine's measurement is a stub. Roadmap: attestation-gated key release to an x86_64 TDX prover.
  3. Operator liveness is a single point of failure. If the sequencer goes down, trading halts. Withdrawals already in a published root stay claimable with their Merkle data, and once close-only begins, deposits that no settled window consumed are refunded from the vault. Other balances and open positions still need the operator/prover and, in final wind-down, governance. There is no failover operator.
  4. Ordering fairness is not yet zk-proven. The proof attests correct execution of the sequenced operations; the sequencing itself is enclave-attested only. A matching-fairness proof (“Proof v2”) is on the roadmap.
  5. Prices and funding inputs are operator-chosen within bounds. The operator's oracle key signs every price the engine uses, including the price at which the final wind-down closes open positions, and the operator chooses which signed price, within the staleness window, each operation carries. The guest bounds funding (rate clamp, mark band, at most one hour charged per gap), but the sequencer chooses the funding mark and the timing of operations. Governance, which in the shipped flow is the hot sequencer key, chooses when close-only starts.
  6. Proof cadence: settles land roughly every 10–20 minutes — one proof per window, with a full-state witness. A sparse-witness redesign (proof cost independent of total account count) is on the roadmap.
  7. The contracts are internally audited (multi-agent adversarial audits; 13 findings remediated pre-alpha) but there is no third-party audit yet.

Between MATCHED and SETTLED, your fills are attested by the TEE and secured economically by the operator's bond — not yet by a zk proof. If that distinction matters to your strategy, wait for SETTLED (finality model).

What secures you in between

  • Operator bond — deposits close at bond capacity. The vault admits a deposit only while 5% of its TVL (requiredBond(), BOND_BPS = 500) stays within the posted bond. Settlement never depends on the bond, so a short bond closes the deposit entrance instead of halting exits.
  • Inclusion challenges: each receipt carries an enclave-signed L1 challenge authorization bound to your deposit address. An hour after the order was received, anyone holding it can open a challenge (stake 0.01 ETH). Anyone can answer it with a settled window's proof. If no answer lands after two further windows settle plus a two-hour grace, the operator's bond pays a capped fine (up to 1,000 USDC on the testnet) to your deposit address, and settlement continues. Opening a challenge publicly links that address to the order hash.
  • Close-only and refunds: if the state root stops progressing for about 24 hours, or a landed deposit stays unconsumed for that long, anyone can put the system into close-only. Unconsumed deposits are then refunded at principal straight from the vault, and settled balances exit through published roots and the governance wind-down.
  • Bad-debt waterfall: a liquidation's shortfall is covered by the insurance fund, then the protocol treasury, then auto-deleveraging of that market's winners, each of which gives at most its gain net of funding and keeps its maintenance margin. Whatever remains switches the system to close-only, after which no new deposit is credited. At wind-down, positions close at each market's last recorded oracle price and the leftover reserves are paid to a recipient address fixed at genesis.

Testing & audit status

  • 351 automated tests across the workspace — engine, sequencer, gateway, and a dedicated Foundry suite for the contracts — including proof-replay merge gates and crash-recovery drills.
  • Internal multi-agent adversarial audits; 13 findings remediated before alpha.
  • No third-party audit yet. One is on the roadmap before mainnet.

Roadmap

  1. TEE-attested zk prover — attestation-gated witness key release to an x86_64 TDX prover.
  2. Sparse-witness proving — proof cost independent of total accounts, for faster settles at scale.
  3. Matching-fairness zk proof (Proof v2) — prove the ordering policy, not just the execution.
  4. Third-party security audit → incentivized testnet → mainnet.

Arcora Perp is a public testnet alpha on Base Sepolia (chain ID 84532). All funds are test funds (open-mint MockUSDC). The contracts have not been audited by a third party — do not treat this as production software.
