Trading

Markets, order types, the house market maker, margin mechanics — and the finality model you must understand before trusting a fill.

Markets

marketIdMarketOracle source
0BTC-PERPcrypto.com exchange ticker
1ETH-PERPcrypto.com exchange ticker

All three are USDC-collateralized perpetual futures traded on a price-time-priority central limit order book. Prices and sizes are fixed-point integers: price and size are scaled by 1e8, quote (USDC) amounts by 1e6 (USDC base units).

Order types

An order is a side (buy/sell), a size, an optional limit price, a time-in-force, and a reduce-only flag.

  • Limit order — executes at your limit price or better. A non-crossing GTC limit rests on the book.
  • Market order — a limit price of 0 means “market”: execute at the prevailing price. Use with IOC or FOK.
Time in forceBehavior
GTCGood-til-canceled. Fills what crosses; the remainder rests on the book until filled or canceled.
IOCImmediate-or-cancel. Fills what it can immediately; any remainder is canceled, nothing rests.
FOKFill-or-kill. Fills completely and immediately, or is rejected in full.
PostOnlyRests as a maker order only. Rejected if it would cross and take immediately.

Reduce-only orders can only decrease your open position in that market — they can never open or grow exposure. Useful for take-profit and stop-style exits.

The house market maker

A gateway-internal “house market maker” — the counterparty backed by the LP pool — quotes at the validated oracle mark against IOC/FOK market orders and crossing limit orders. Practically, this means takers always have a counterparty at the oracle price even when the resting book is thin. GTC limit orders rest on the book as usual and match against other traders under price-time priority.

The LP pool is open: you can stake USDC into the market maker's equity pool and hold shares of its PnL (see the LP endpoints).

Leverage, liquidation, insurance, ADL

  • Leverage is margin-based: your USDC balance collateralizes your positions, and position size is bounded by margin requirements checked at order time.
  • Liquidation closes under-margined positions.
  • An insurance fund (seeded with 25,000 USDC) absorbs losses that liquidations cannot cover.
  • If the insurance fund is insufficient, auto-deleveraging (ADL) trims the most profitable opposing positions. ADL haircuts are delivered as receipts that are unlinkable to third parties — outsiders cannot tell whose positions were trimmed.

Liquidations, funding, and ADL are part of the proven engine transition — a settled state root attests they were applied exactly per the rules, not at the operator's discretion. See Architecture.

The finality model: ACCEPTED → MATCHED → SETTLED

Arcora Perp separates speed from certainty. Every order moves through three states, and they are not interchangeable:

StateWhenWhat it means
ACCEPTED milliseconds The enclave took your order and returned a signed receipt (secp256k1, recovering to the enclave signer). The receipt is your inclusion proof: if the operator later settles a window that omits your order, you can open an on-chain challenge with it and slash the operator's bond.
MATCHED milliseconds Your order filled inside the enclave. This is soft finality: the fill is real in the engine's state and the app shows it immediately, but it is not yet proven on-chain. It is backed by the TEE's integrity and the operator's bond — not yet by a zk proof.
SETTLED ~10–20 min The settlement window containing your fill was re-executed inside the SP1 zkVM, and the Groth16 proof was verified by the settlement contract on Base Sepolia. This is hard finality: the state transition is now cryptographically attested and cannot be forged or rolled back by the operator.

MATCHED is not SETTLED

A MATCHED fill is a millisecond-latency preconfirmation, not an on-chain fact. Until the window containing it is SETTLED (~10–20 minutes later), the fill is attested only by the TEE and secured economically by the operator's bond. Only settled state is trustlessly withdrawable. If your strategy assumes finality — for example, withdrawing profits or bridging out — wait for SETTLED.

What is withdrawable, when

  1. You request a withdrawal (POST /v1/accounts/withdraw or the Account tab). Every withdrawal is wallet-signed: it must carry a strictly-increasing nonce and a secp256k1 signature from your bound deposit address (or the registered signer, for caller-signed accounts) — the API key alone can never move funds, and a server-custody account can only withdraw to its bound deposit address (so keep that key safe: there is no operator recovery for a lost bound key). See the API reference for the exact digest. The withdrawal is recorded off-chain and enters the current settlement window.
  2. When that window's proof lands on-chain, the withdrawal is included in the window's on-chain withdrawals Merkle root.
  3. From that moment it is claimable by anyone, permissionlessly, via claim(to, amount, nonce, root, proof) on the vault — funds can only go to the address bound into the leaf. Any historically published root remains claimable forever; a later root can never strand a withdrawal.

So the practical rule: a withdrawal takes up to one proof interval (~10–20 minutes) to become claimable. Track it via GET /v1/accounts/withdrawals (returns claim proofs) or the Account tab's status.

Watching finality

  • GET /v1/orders — your orders with their current finality state.
  • GET /v1/batch/:id — {windowId, settled} for a settlement window.
  • GET /v1/system/status — overall system status, including settlement progress.
